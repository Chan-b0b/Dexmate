\section{An Instrument for Causal Authority}
\label{sec:method}

Our instrument has two halves: a conditioning pathway constructed so that interventions on it are
meaningful (\ref{sec:method-chat}--\ref{sec:method-mask}), and a battery of counterfactual probes
that perform the interventions (\ref{sec:method-probes}). Throughout, the design goal is
\emph{measurement validity}, not architectural novelty: every component exists to make one number
well-defined---how much does the condition causally move the action?

\subsection{Computed condition vector \chat{}}
\label{sec:method-chat}
From the wrench $F$ and seal bit already present in the state we compute a small set of
task-critical conditions---the physical cues a human operator references (``if you feel contact,
stop; if the seal is on, lift''):
\begin{align}
\chat_{\mathrm{contact}} &= \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\left(\Fmag - F_0\right)/\tau,\ 0,\ 1\right),\nonumber\\
\chat_{\mathrm{fmag}} &= \left(\Fmag - F_0\right)/\tau_m \quad\text{(unsaturated)},\nonumber\\
\chat_{f_z} &= \left(f_z - o_z\right)/\tau_z,\qquad
\chat_{\mathrm{seal}} = \mathrm{seal},
\label{eq:chat}
\end{align}
with offsets and scales calibrated to the measured force statistics of the demonstrations (e.g.,
$F_0$ at the contact-onset magnitude).\footnote{Calibration quality matters and is itself a
finding: the four-model matrix of Sec.~\ref{sec:offline-bypass} uses an earlier, coarser
calibration; the matched quintuple of Sec.~\ref{sec:offline-quintuple} uses offsets re-estimated
from the measured contact distribution plus the unsaturated $\chat_{\mathrm{fmag}}$ channel. An
uncalibrated transplant onto a different architecture fails outright
(Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).} Two properties are essential. \emph{Computed, not learned:} \chat{}
is a fixed function of observed signals, so it cannot itself be bypassed during training the way
a learned condition head could. \emph{Zero new information:} every channel is a deterministic
function of state dimensions the baseline also receives, so conditioning changes
\emph{routing}, never \emph{content}---the ``it works because it got extra sensing'' objection is
excluded by construction. The channels are deliberately plural: keeping contact, force magnitude,
and seal separate is what later lets the probes attribute authority to specific signals
(Sec.~\ref{sec:offline-posthoc}).

\subsection{Zero-initialized FiLM injection}
\chat{} enters the policy through FiLM modulation~\cite{perez2018film}, with the modulation
initialized to identity so that training starts from the unconditioned policy. We instantiate two
injection points in SmolVLA: \emph{prefix} (modulating the state token entering the VLM backbone)
and \emph{suffix} (modulating the action-expert input). The comparison is not incidental---the
two sites differ consistently in the authority they acquire (Sec.~\ref{sec:offline-bypass}).

\subsection{The force-mask bottleneck}
\label{sec:method-mask}
With \chat{} injected but the raw wrench still present in the state, the policy has two routes to
force. Masking the wrench dimensions from the state closes the raw route, making \chat{} the
\emph{only} pathway from force to action. The mask is the instrument's validity device: under it,
forcing \chat{} intervenes on the totality of the policy's force access, so measured authority is
a property of the policy's force use, not of one redundant pathway among several. It also
strictly \emph{reduces} the conditioned model's information relative to the baseline. The
unmasked configuration is retained as a control---and turns out to demonstrate bypass
(Sec.~\ref{sec:offline-bypass}).

\subsection{Counterfactual probe battery}
\label{sec:method-probes}
All probes intervene on a trained, frozen policy; none involves retraining. We define
\textbf{authority} as the intervention-induced change in the commanded vertical action on
committed-descent frames, expressed as a percentage of the mean commanded descent: $0\%$ means
the intervention is ignored, $100\%$ means it fully cancels descent, ${>}100\%$ means reversal
(retreat).

\textbf{(P1) Condition forcing (open loop).} With images and state frozen, \chat{} is forced from
its observed value to a target pattern---either all channels saturated (\emph{full forcing}) or a
calibrated contact-onset pattern matching measured first-contact statistics (\emph{contact-moment
forcing}). Applicable to conditioned models only.

\textbf{(P2) State transplant (open loop).} The force-bearing dimensions of a \emph{measured}
first-contact state are transplanted into pre-contact descent frames of held-out episodes. This
probe applies to any policy, including the baseline, and supports a pathway decomposition: apply
the transplant to the raw state only ($d_{\mathrm{raw}}$), to the \chat{} computation only
($d_{\mathrm{FiLM}}$), or to both (total), attributing the response to the raw route versus the
conditioning route. \todo{verify exact transplanted dims (wrench+seal vs full state) in
probe\_state\_authority.py before submission}

\textbf{(P3) Dose-response sweep.} The transplanted wrench is rescaled to target magnitudes
($8$, $10$, $12$\,N), tracing the policy's response as a function of force \emph{dose}. The
demonstrations peak near the low end of this range, so the sweep measures extrapolation: a
policy that has bound braking to force should brake \emph{more} at higher dose; one that has
memorized a contact template should respond most near the template and fade beyond it. The
sweep's \emph{slope sign} is the paper's central discriminator.

\textbf{(P4) Closed-loop press simulation.} Open-loop probes cannot show consequences. We place
the frozen policy in a closed loop on held-out episodes: commanded descent advances a virtual
penetration depth, a linear-stiffness force model raises the simulated wrench accordingly, and
the seal event is withheld---simulating the operationally critical misalignment scenario in
which no press depth ever produces a seal. Outcomes: whether the policy stops before the force
limit, and maximum penetration. A control variant grants the seal at a shallow depth,
separating force-driven stopping from seal-event-driven stopping.

\textbf{(P5) Live counterfactual probe.} The full loop contains physics no offline probe
reproduces. On the robot, we freeze observations at staged poses along the approach and apply
(P1)--(P3) interventions to \emph{two} policies---the conditioned model and the baseline---on
identical frozen observations, so both receive the same physical counterfactual. Two operational
details matter for validity: force/torque baseline drift is re-anchored at a pre-descent hover
pose before probing (drift of up to ${\sim}1$\,N within a day was measured, pose-sensitive), and
absolute transplant targets are shifted by the measured drift so that both models receive the
same effective dose.

\textbf{Scope.} P1--P3 are single-frame and open-loop; P4 closes the loop under a force model;
P5 closes the gap to the real system. No single probe is decisive---the findings below rest on
their agreement.
