Vision-language-action (VLA) policies increasingly receive physical condition signals---wrist
wrench, contact, vacuum-seal state---in their observations, yet whether a policy \emph{causally
uses} them is unmeasured. On a real-robot suction case-picking task whose critical
transition---stop descending on contact---is observable only through force (single head camera;
case heights are visually near-indistinguishable), a fine-tuned VLA that receives the full wrench
presses through contact until a safety abort in every trial. We introduce \emph{counterfactual
authority probes}: a computed condition vector \chat{} (contact, normal force, seal) injected
through zero-initialized FiLM with the raw wrench masked, so that \chat{} is the only force
pathway and the conditioned policy receives \emph{no information the baseline lacks}. Forcing
\chat{} at inference measures how much the condition actually moves the action. The probes expose
a \emph{bypass} phenomenon: with the wrench unmasked, conditioning trains to validation loss
identical to the fourth decimal yet near-zero authority---a difference invisible to standard
validation.
A matched quintuple (same data, same initialization, indistinguishable imitation error) shows the
architecture does not force usage but \emph{determines its form}: raw access yields a template
response that fades as force exceeds the demonstrations, an ungrounded bottleneck yields none, and
only the grounded bottleneck brakes monotonically with force---stopping and retreating at
$12$\,N, converging in all closed-loop press simulations where the alternatives penetrate. On the
robot, force-decorrelated press-retreat demonstrations plus the grounded bottleneck convert $0/3$
over-press failures into $5/7$ gentle picks ($2.5$--$4.8$\,N), and the dose-response crossover
reproduces on-robot. The failure class persists unchanged in a $3.6$B-parameter VLA.
