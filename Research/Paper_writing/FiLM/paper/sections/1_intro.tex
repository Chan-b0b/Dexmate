\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Behavior-cloned vision-language-action (VLA) policies are rapidly acquiring physical condition
signals. Wrist force/torque, contact events, and task-state bits now routinely appear in the
observation vector, and a growing line of work fuses force into the policy backbone to improve
contact-rich manipulation~\cite{forcevla,phaforce,forceflow,filic}. All of this work shares a
validation recipe: add the signal, retrain, report task success. What none of it measures is
whether the trained policy \emph{causally uses} the signal at the moment that matters---whether,
holding everything else fixed, changing the force input changes the action. Imitation learning
gives no such guarantee. The objective pins down what the expert did along the data manifold; it
leaves \emph{why}---which input the behavior is bound to---undetermined, to be settled by whatever
correlations the demonstrations happen to contain~\cite{dehaan2019causal,geirhos2020shortcut}.
\emph{Imitation pins down the trajectory, not the mechanism: equally faithful clones can act from
opposite causes, and the loss cannot tell them apart.}

We study this question in a deliberately clean diagnostic setting: real-robot suction case
picking, where the critical transition---stop descending when the suction cup meets the
case---is observable \emph{only} through force. The robot has a single head-mounted camera and no
wrist camera; case-stack heights that differ by a few millimeters, the margin between a seal and
a crushed case, are visually near-indistinguishable at head-camera range. The force signal is in
the state the policy already receives. Yet a SmolVLA policy fine-tuned on $100$ demonstrations
presses through contact until a $15$\,N safety abort in $3/3$ trials: the one transition the task
is about is exactly the one the policy does not gate on force.

To measure this---rather than infer it from success rates---we build an \emph{instrument}. A
condition vector \chat{} (contact, normal-force magnitude, seal) is \emph{computed} from signals
the baseline policy already observes and injected through zero-initialized FiLM
modulation~\cite{perez2018film}; masking the raw wrench from the state makes \chat{} the
\emph{only} pathway through which force can reach the action. By construction the conditioned
policy receives no information the baseline lacks---the design changes routing, not content---so
any behavioral difference is attributable to representation, and forcing \chat{} at inference
yields a direct measurement of \emph{causal authority}: the fraction of the descent command that
the condition can cancel. Around this bottleneck we assemble a probe battery---open-loop condition
forcing, measured-state transplants with pathway decomposition, force dose-response sweeps,
closed-loop press simulation, and on-robot frozen-observation counterfactuals
(Sec.~\ref{sec:method}).

The probes expose a failure mode that standard validation cannot see, and a design axis that
standard validation cannot credit. First, \emph{bypass}: when the raw wrench is left in the state,
the identical FiLM conditioning trains to a validation loss equal to the baseline's in the fourth
decimal place, yet its condition channels carry near-zero authority---the policy routes around
them. Training loss is blind to causal use, and across our model family loss rank is
\emph{anti}-correlated with authority rank (Sec.~\ref{sec:offline}). Second, \emph{form
determination}: in a matched quintuple---same data, same initialization, imitation error
indistinguishable at $0.8$--$1.0$\,mm---the policies differ radically under intervention. The raw
baseline responds to force at the demonstrated magnitude but its response \emph{fades} as force
rises beyond it; a bottleneck fed shuffled (ungrounded) conditions responds to nothing and is the
worst policy in closed loop; only the grounded bottleneck responds \emph{monotonically}, braking
harder the harder it is pressed, up to full stop and retreat at $12$\,N---beyond its training
range. The architecture cannot force the policy to use force (that is decided by the data's
correlation structure), but it \emph{determines the form} force-use takes when the data permits
it. On the robot, demonstrations that decorrelate force from its habitual correlates
(randomized press-retreat) plus the grounded bottleneck convert $0/3$ over-press failures into
$5/7$ gentle picks at $2.5$--$4.8$\,N contact force, and the dose-response crossover between
baseline and bottleneck reproduces in on-robot counterfactuals. The failure class is not a
small-model artifact: a $3.6$B-parameter $\pi_{0.5}$ policy shows the same template signature and
worse closed-loop collapse (Sec.~\ref{sec:offline}).

Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Diagnosis.} On a task whose critical transition is force-observable only, we show a
  BC-fine-tuned VLA with full access to the signal does not gate the transition on it, failing
  $3/3$ by over-press; closed-loop simulation shows its stopping is bound to correlated events
  (seal), not to force (Sec.~\ref{sec:task},~\ref{sec:offline}).
\item \textbf{Instrument.} A zero-new-information conditioning bottleneck (computed \chat{},
  zero-init FiLM, force mask) plus a counterfactual probe battery that measures moment-resolved
  causal authority, decomposes it by pathway and by channel, and transfers to the real robot
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{Findings.} Bypass and loss-blindness, replicated in two model pairs to the fourth
  decimal of validation loss; authority binds to stable post-hoc signals rather than the contact
  transient; and the usage/form dissociation established by the matched quintuple with a
  dose-response slope-sign reversal (Sec.~\ref{sec:offline}).
\item \textbf{Prescription and validation.} Force-decorrelated press-retreat demonstrations as
  the data-side fix; on-robot conversion of $0/3$ to $5/7$ with an order-of-magnitude contact
  force reduction; live counterfactual reproduction of the offline mechanism; architecture
  generality on $\pi_{0.5}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:robot}).
\end{itemize}

\noindent In one sentence: \emph{behavior-cloned VLA policies silently bypass the causal contact
signal they are given; training loss cannot detect this; and while no architecture can force the
signal to be used, a grounded conditioning bottleneck determines the form its use takes---turning
the same demonstrations into a force-monotone brake instead of a fading template.}
