\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Behavior-cloned vision-language-action (VLA) policies are rapidly acquiring physical condition
signals. Wrist force/torque, contact events, and task-state bits now routinely appear in the
observation vector, and a growing line of work fuses force into the policy backbone for
contact-rich manipulation~\cite{forcevla,phaforce,forceflow,filic}. All of this work shares a
validation recipe---add the signal, retrain, report task success---and none of it measures
whether the trained policy \emph{causally uses} the signal when it matters: whether, holding
everything else fixed, changing the force input changes the action. Imitation learning offers no
such guarantee. The objective pins down what the expert did along the data manifold; \emph{which
input the behavior is bound to} is left to whatever correlations the demonstrations happen to
contain~\cite{dehaan2019causal,geirhos2020shortcut}.

The consequence is easiest to see where force is the only signal that can carry the task. We
study real-robot suction case picking with a single head-mounted camera and no wrist camera: the
arm must stop descending the moment the suction cup meets the case, and stack heights differing
by a few millimeters---the entire success margin---are visually near-indistinguishable at
head-camera range. The wrench is in the state the policy receives, and every demonstration
exhibits the stop. Yet a naively fine-tuned VLA---full wrench in its state, hereafter the
\emph{naive policy}---presses through contact in every evaluation trial and never initiates a
stop of its own: each run ends only when an external safety layer, separate
from the policy, trips at its $15$\,N force limit ($15.4$--$18.7$\,N measured at abort)---the
policy still commanding descent. The task turns on a single transition---stop when force
rises---and it is precisely this transition that the policy fails to bind to force.

This failure leaves no trace in training. A policy that binds its actions to force and a policy
that routes around it can reach the same validation loss---in our experiments, equal to the
\emph{fourth decimal place}---so no loss value can certify a fix. We call this routing-around
\emph{bypass} and exhibit it directly in Sec.~\ref{sec:offline}. A fix must therefore hold
\emph{by construction}: leave the policy no route around the signal, and make its use of the
signal directly measurable.

Our prescription does both. A condition vector \chat{} (contact, normal force, seal) is
\emph{computed} from signals the naive policy already receives and injected through zero-initialized
FiLM modulation~\cite{perez2018film}, and the raw wrench is masked from the state, so \chat{}
becomes the \emph{only} pathway from force to action---no route around. We call this design a
\emph{grounded bottleneck}---a bottleneck because \chat{} is the sole route from force to
action, grounded because its content is computed from measured signals---and the policy trained
behind it the \emph{conditioned policy}. By construction the
conditioned policy receives no information the naive policy lacks; the design changes routing, not
content. And because the pathway is explicit, forcing \chat{} at inference directly measures its
\emph{causal authority}---the fraction of commanded descent the condition can cancel. The
demonstrations complete the recipe: each press-retreat episode presses to a randomized force
target and retreats before any seal event, so the force at which the expert stops varies from
episode to episode---in the data, depth, appearance, and timing no longer predict the stop;
only the force reading does. The same interface that fixes the
policy is the instrument that audits it (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}).

The resulting policy is not uniformly better---it is different exactly where the task is
decided. In a matched comparison---same data, same initialization, imitation error
indistinguishable---the naive policy \emph{does} respond to force at the demonstrated
magnitudes, as strongly as the conditioned policy or more so. But its response is a
\emph{template}: strongest near what the demonstrations felt, fading as force rises past them.
The conditioned policy's response instead grows with force, to the point of stopping and
backing away at forces beyond anything in the training data (Sec.~\ref{sec:offline}). The same
comparison doubles as an ablation of the recipe: remove the mask and the pathway trains dead
(bypass); remove the grounding and the policy becomes the worst in the study. On the robot, the difference
appears as \emph{force regulation}: the naive policy's contact forces are censored at the
external abort ($15$\,N$+$---lower bounds, not chosen stopping forces), the conditioned
policy's sit at $2.5$--$4.8$\,N---an order of magnitude below---and live counterfactual probes
reproduce the offline crossover on the physical system (Sec.~\ref{sec:robot}). The failure
class also survives scale: a $3.6$B-parameter $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{pi05} fine-tuned on the same
demonstrations shows the same fading-template signature (Sec.~\ref{sec:offline-pi05}).

Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A recipe for making a VLA use force when it matters.} Force-decorrelated
  press-retreat demonstrations plus a grounded conditioning bottleneck (computed \chat{},
  zero-initialized FiLM, raw-wrench mask) turn the same demonstrations into a force-monotone
  brake instead of a fading template---on the robot, regulating contact force an order of
  magnitude below the safety limit on a transition the naive policy fails uniformly
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:method},~\ref{sec:robot}).
\item \textbf{An instrument for causal authority.} The bottleneck is measurable by construction:
  a counterfactual probe battery---condition forcing, state transplants with pathway
  decomposition, dose-response sweeps, closed-loop press simulation, and on-robot
  frozen-observation counterfactuals---quantifies moment-resolved causal use and transfers to
  the real system (Sec.~\ref{sec:method-probes}).
\item \textbf{Findings standard validation cannot produce.} \emph{Bypass}: with the raw signal
  available, the conditioning pathway trains causally dead at indistinguishable validation
  loss---replicated in two independent model pairs---so no quantity in the standard stack
  detects whether force is used. A matched-quintuple ablation shows each ingredient of the
  recipe is necessary (mask, grounding, injection site), and dissociates the roles: the data's
  correlation structure decides \emph{whether} a signal is used; the conditioning design decides
  the \emph{form} its use takes under extrapolation. The failure class persists unchanged at
  $3.6$B scale (Sec.~\ref{sec:offline}).
\end{itemize}

\noindent In one sentence: \emph{a policy that stops when it feels what the demonstrations felt
and a policy that stops harder the harder it is pressed are indistinguishable in loss and
similar at the demonstrated forces---they differ exactly where the task is decided, and only
force-decorrelated data behind a grounded, masked FiLM bottleneck yields the second.}
