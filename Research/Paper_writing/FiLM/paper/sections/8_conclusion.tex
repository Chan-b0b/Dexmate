\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On a picking task where force must govern when the descent ends, a behavior-cloned VLA policy
given the full wrench did not let that signal govern its behavior, pressing on until an
external safety limit intervened. We changed how force is delivered, not what is delivered: a
condition vector computed from signals the policy already receives, fed to the backbone
through a FiLM layer. The pathway is drop-in, attaches to an already-trained policy, carries to
another backbone, and---because it applies force magnitude through affine modulation---brakes
harder the harder it is pressed, stopping and retreating beyond the demonstrated forces and
succeeding at an undemonstrated stack height. Because the condition is explicit, the same
pathway can be interrogated: forcing it at inference reads out how much, and in what form, the
policy responds to force. From the same demonstrations, backbone, and signal, how force enters
the policy decided what the policy learned to do with it.
