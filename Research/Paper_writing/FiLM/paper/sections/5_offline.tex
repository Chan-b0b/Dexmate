\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:offline}

We present results in the order of the probe battery: P1--P4 offline (this section), then the
closed-loop picks and the live probe P5 on the robot
(Sec.~\ref{sec:robot}--\ref{sec:robot-live}). Every reported model is its
validation-loss-selected (\emph{best}) checkpoint---one uniform selection rule for all
policies---and the robot deploys the same checkpoints.

\subsection{Ablating the recipe, probe by probe}
\label{sec:offline-quintuple}

The central experiment removes the recipe's ingredients one at a time while holding everything
else fixed. Starting from the \emph{same} trained naive checkpoint, on the \emph{same}
demonstrations, we continue training four policies: the naive policy itself; FiLM
\emph{without} the mask (restores the second route to force); FiLM with mask but
\emph{shuffled} \chat{} during training (removes the grounding: the pathway exists, its
content is noise); and the full recipe. Imitation error on held-out episodes is
indistinguishable across all four ($0.84$--$0.96$\,mm per step): on the data manifold, these
are the same policy. Under intervention they are three different mechanisms
(Table~\ref{tab:quintuple}, Fig.~\ref{fig:doseresponse}); the probes below walk the table
column by column.

\textbf{(P1) Condition forcing: the pathway is live in exactly one policy.} Forcing every
channel of \chat{} to saturation moves the grounded policy's committed descent by
$+0.9$\,mm per frame; the identical forcing moves the unmasked variant by $0.0$ and the
shuffled variant by $-0.2$ (Table~\ref{tab:quintuple}). Of the three policies that own the
pathway, only the grounded one carries force through it to the action. Restricting the forcing
to a calibrated contact-onset pattern shows the limit of what any policy adopts: even the
best-conditioned policy moves by only $7\%$---the 1--2-frame contact \emph{transient} is
weakly adopted across the board, a point Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} returns to.

\textbf{(P2) State transplant: both policies on one scale, and the bypass exposed.}
\label{sec:offline-posthoc}
Transplanting a measured first-contact state into pre-contact descent frames moves the naive
policy by $+1.63$\,mm---$105\%$ of its descent command. The naive policy is not force-blind:
its raw route responds strongly to force-bearing state. The unmasked variant responds
identically ($+1.68$\,mm), but the pathway decomposition places its response entirely on the
raw route ($d_{\mathrm{FiLM}}{=}0.04$): with a redundant raw route available, gradient descent
satisfies the imitation objective through the already-functional path and starves the
dedicated pathway. This is the \emph{bypass} of Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}---and nothing in training
registers it: the unmasked variant's validation loss matches the grounded variant's to the
fourth decimal ($0.14750$ vs.\ $0.14762$). An independently trained pair under the earlier
calibration reproduces the result: $7\%$ condition-forcing authority with the wrench kept
versus $76\%$ with it masked, again at near-identical loss. In the grounded policy the same
transplant lands entirely on $d_{\mathrm{FiLM}}$ ($+1.57$\,mm, $94\%$)---the mask working as
designed.

\emph{What} is transplanted matters as much as whether: a \emph{sealed} state moves both
models far more than the contact state does (on-robot: $147\%$ vs.\ $67\%$ for the naive
policy; Sec.~\ref{sec:robot-live}). Together with the weak contact-onset forcing above, the
pattern is that stable, persistent conditions (seal, sustained press) are adopted essentially
for free, while a transient that is redundant with depth-like cues in ordinary demonstrations
is not. The operational corollary is developed in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}: on stiff contact,
even substantial condition authority cannot succeed by \emph{reactive} gating at the contact
moment---the viable mechanism is an anticipatory behavior prior plus post-hoc state gating,
which is exactly what the probes measure as adopted.

\textbf{(P3) Dose-response: raw access binds a template, the grounded pathway binds a brake.}
The naive policy responds to transplanted force---at the demonstrated magnitude. As the dose
rises past the demonstrations, its response \emph{fades} ($36\%\rightarrow28\%$): a template,
matched ever more poorly as reality departs from it. The unmasked variant's curve lies on top
of the naive policy's ($+1.57\rightarrow+1.12$ vs.\ $+1.41\rightarrow+1.12$; identical at
$12$\,N)---adding the FiLM module without the mask changes nothing about how force reaches
the action. The ungrounded pathway is flat at zero at every dose. Only the grounded pathway
\emph{grows} with dose, crossing full cancellation into commanded retreat at $12$\,N
($31\%\rightarrow115\%$)---where the unsaturated channel reads five times its training range.
The monotone extrapolation is not learned from data (no demonstration presses at these
forces); it is inherited from the channel's design as a calibrated monotone scalar. The forms
are not artifacts of checkpoint selection: no naive checkpoint we examined exceeds $65\%$
cancellation at any dose, while the grounded brake remains monotone to the end of training.
Same data, same imitation: raw access binds a template, the grounded pathway binds a brake.

\textbf{(P4) Press simulation: consequences, and the ungrounded control.} Closing the loop
under the seal-never press turns the response forms into outcomes: the grounded policy stops
before the force limit in $6/6$ episodes (max penetration $4.5$\,mm); the naive and unmasked
policies press to the limit in at least one episode each (max $14.9$ and $11.9$\,mm); the
shuffled control collapses ($0/6$, max $431$\,mm). The shuffled row is the study's sharpest
control: same added parameters, same continued training, same pathway---and zero response at
every dose with the worst closed-loop outcome. Grounding, not capacity, is the active
ingredient, and an ungrounded pathway is \emph{worse} than raw access: the mask removed the
raw route and the noise-trained \chat{} replaced it with nothing. Two further observations
localize the naive policy's stopping. Later naive checkpoints respond \emph{more} in open
loop yet fare \emph{worse} here, because a response that saturates below full cancellation
cannot stop a stiff press regardless of its size. And the seal-granted control variant shows
the naive policy stopping normally ($0.5$\,mm over-travel) when a seal event arrives at
shallow depth---withhold the seal and it presses through. Its stopping is real but bound to
the \emph{seal event}; the grounded policy's is bound to \emph{force}.

\subsection{Scale does not change the class}
\label{sec:offline-pi05}

Fine-tuning $\pi_{0.5}$ ($3.6$B parameters, $8\times$ SmolVLA)~\cite{pi05} on the same
demonstrations reproduces the naive-policy signature: strong on-manifold transplant response
($65\%$), a dose-response that saturates shallowly (never exceeding $37\%$ cancellation,
approaching no full stop at any dose), and closed-loop collapse in the seal-never press
simulation ($0/6$, max penetration $86$\,mm---worse than the smaller model), at equal-or-better
imitation error. Neither parameter count nor a different VLA architecture converts template
responding into force-bound braking; the failure class is a property of the objective-plus-data,
not of model scale.
