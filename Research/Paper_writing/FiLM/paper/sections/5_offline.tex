\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:offline}

We present the offline probe battery first
(\ref{sec:offline-bypass}--\ref{sec:offline-pi05}), then the robot validation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:robot}--\ref{sec:robot-live}). All offline probes run on the six held-out
validation episodes. Checkpoints are selected by validation loss (\emph{best}); the final
checkpoint (\emph{last}) is reported where it qualifies the story.

\subsection{Bypass: standard validation cannot see causal use}
\label{sec:offline-bypass}

policies trained identically on the same data.

Three observations. \textbf{(i) Bypass.} With the wrench left in the state, the FiLM-conditioned
policy trains to a validation loss equal to the baseline's in the \emph{fourth decimal place}
(0.15075 vs.\ 0.15087) while its condition channels carry $7$--$8\%$ authority: gradient descent
satisfied the imitation objective through the raw route and left the dedicated conditioning
pathway causally dead. Nothing in the training signal distinguishes this policy from one that
uses its conditions. \textbf{(ii) Loss rank anti-correlates with authority rank.} The masked
prefix model has both the \emph{lowest} loss and the highest authority; the suffix model has the
\emph{worst} loss yet $60\%$ authority; the unmasked model sits between them in loss with none.
A practitioner selecting on loss learns nothing about causal use (Fig.~\ref{fig:loss-authority}).
\textbf{(iii) Selection and site effects.} Validation-loss checkpoint selection also selects for
authority (best $>$ last in every conditioned model), and prefix injection dominates suffix. The
bypass result replicates in an independent pair below (Sec.~\ref{sec:offline-quintuple}):
warm-started variants with and without the mask again differ by $10^{-4}$ in loss and
categorically in authority.

\subsection{Where authority binds: stable states, not the transient}
\label{sec:offline-posthoc}

Authority, where it exists, is not uniformly distributed over the condition channels.
Contact-moment forcing (P1, calibrated onset pattern) yields only $7$--$10\%$ even for the
best-conditioned model (Table~\ref{tab:bypass}, last column): the 1--2-frame contact
\emph{transient} is weakly adopted across the board. The state-transplant probe (P2) shows what
is adopted instead. Transplanting a measured first-contact state into pre-contact descent frames
moves the \emph{baseline} by $105\%$ of its descent command---the baseline is not force-blind;
its raw route responds strongly to force-bearing state. But transplanting a \emph{sealed} state
moves both models far more than the contact state does (on-robot: $147\%$ vs.\ $67\%$ for the
baseline; Sec.~\ref{sec:robot}), and the strongest single responses everywhere are to the
stable, sustained signals---seal and sustained press---rather than to contact onset.
\todo{add contact-z distribution analysis from 0729 dataset (bimodal contact heights) ---
derivation pending, Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp} placeholder}

The picture: what a policy adopts is decided by the \emph{statistical character} of the signal.
Stable, persistent conditions (seal state) are adopted essentially for free; a transient that is
redundant with depth-like cues in ordinary demonstrations is not. This has an operational
corollary developed in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}: on stiff contact, even a policy with
substantial condition authority cannot succeed by \emph{reactive} gating at the contact
moment---the successful mechanism is anticipatory behavior plus post-hoc state gating, which is
exactly what the probes measure as adopted.

\subsection{The matched quintuple: architecture determines the form of force use}
\label{sec:offline-quintuple}

The section's central experiment holds everything fixed except the conditioning pathway. Starting
from the \emph{same} trained baseline checkpoint, on the \emph{same} demonstrations, we continue
training four variants: the baseline itself, FiLM \emph{without} the mask (bypass control), FiLM
with mask but \emph{shuffled} \chat{} during training (ungrounded control: the bottleneck exists,
its content is noise), and FiLM with mask and grounded \chat{}. Imitation error on held-out
episodes is indistinguishable across all five policies ($0.81$--$0.96$\,mm per step): on the data
manifold, these are the same policy. Under intervention they are three different mechanisms
(Table~\ref{tab:quintuple}, Fig.~\ref{fig:doseresponse}).

\textbf{Grounding, not capacity, is the active ingredient.} The ungrounded control kills every
alternative explanation at once: it has the same added parameters, the same continued training,
the same bottleneck---and zero response at every dose, with the worst closed-loop outcome in the
study ($431$\,mm penetration). An ungrounded bottleneck is \emph{worse} than raw access: the mask
removed the raw route and the noise-trained \chat{} replaced it with nothing. Conversely, the
unmasked control shows that adding the FiLM module without the mask changes nothing: its
dose-response curve lies on the baseline's ($+1.57\rightarrow+1.12$ vs.\ $+1.41\rightarrow+1.12$;
identical at $12$\,N), its pathway decomposition puts the response entirely on the raw route, and
its loss matches the grounded variant's to the fourth decimal ($0.14750$ vs.\ $0.14762$)---the
bypass finding, replicated with authority now at $94\%$ vs.\ $0$.

\textbf{The form dissociation.} The baseline responds to force---at the demonstrated magnitude.
As the dose rises past the demonstrations, its response \emph{fades} ($36\%\rightarrow28\%$): a
template, matched ever more poorly as reality departs from it. The grounded bottleneck's response
\emph{grows monotonically}, crossing full cancellation into retreat at $12$\,N---where the
unsaturated channel reads five times its training range. The monotone extrapolation is not
learned from data (no demonstration presses to these forces); it is inherited from the channel's
design as a calibrated monotone scalar. Same data, same imitation: raw access binds a template,
the grounded bottleneck binds a brake.

\textbf{Honest boundaries of the discriminator.} The baseline's \emph{shape} is
checkpoint-dependent: at $50$k steps its curve rises mildly ($57\%\rightarrow65\%$). The robust
discriminators are therefore not slope sign alone but (i) slope \emph{magnitude}
(${\sim}10\times$: $+2.4$--$2.5$\,mm vs.\ $\pm0.3$\,mm over the sweep), (ii) whether the curve
\emph{crosses} full cancellation (only the grounded models stop and retreat; no baseline
checkpoint exceeds $65\%$), and (iii) the closed-loop consequence---the late baseline's larger
open-loop response coexists with a \emph{worse} press-sim outcome ($3/6$, max $16.0$\,mm),
because a response that saturates below $100\%$ cannot stop a stiff press regardless of its
size. The grounded form is stable across checkpoints ($6/6$ at both). Finally, the seal-granted
control variant of P4 localizes the baseline's failure: given a seal event at shallow depth it
stops normally ($0.5$\,mm over-travel); withhold the seal and it presses through. Its stopping
is real but bound to the \emph{seal event}; the grounded policy's is bound to \emph{force}.

\subsection{Scale does not change the class}
\label{sec:offline-pi05}

Fine-tuning $\pi_{0.5}$ ($3.6$B parameters, $8\times$ SmolVLA)~\cite{pi05} on the same
demonstrations reproduces the baseline signature: strong on-manifold transplant response
($65$--$97\%$), a dose-response that saturates shallowly (never exceeding $37\%$ cancellation,
approaching no full stop at any dose), and closed-loop collapse in the seal-never press
simulation ($0/6$, max penetration $86$\,mm---worse than the smaller model), at equal-or-better
imitation error ($0.69$--$0.89$\,mm). Neither parameter count nor a different VLA architecture
converts template responding into force-bound braking; the failure class is a property of the
objective-plus-data, not of model scale.
