\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:offline}

We present the offline probe battery first
(\ref{sec:offline-bypass}--\ref{sec:offline-pi05}), then the robot validation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:robot}--\ref{sec:robot-live}). All offline probes run on the six held-out
validation episodes, and every reported model is its validation-loss-selected (\emph{best})
checkpoint---one uniform selection rule for all policies; the robot deployments below use the
same checkpoints.

\subsection{Bypass: standard validation cannot see causal use}
\label{sec:offline-bypass}

Table~\ref{tab:bypass} varies wrench masking for FiLM-conditioned policies trained identically
on the same data.

 & & full forcing & contact-moment \\
\midrule

\bottomrule

Two observations. \textbf{(i) Bypass.} With the wrench left in the state, the FiLM-conditioned
policy trains to a validation loss equal to the naive policy's in the \emph{fourth decimal
place} (0.15075 vs.\ 0.15087) while its condition channels carry $7\%$ authority: gradient
descent satisfied the imitation objective through the raw route and left the dedicated
conditioning pathway causally dead. Nothing in the training signal distinguishes this policy
from one that uses its conditions. \textbf{(ii) The mask reroutes, at no cost.} Masking the
wrench makes the same conditioning pathway carry $76\%$ authority---and yields the
\emph{lowest} validation loss of the three policies: closing the raw route costs nothing on
the manifold. The bypass result replicates in an independent pair below
(Sec.~\ref{sec:offline-quintuple}): variants with and without the mask again differ by
$10^{-4}$ in loss and categorically in authority.

\subsection{Where authority binds: stable states, not the transient}
\label{sec:offline-posthoc}

Authority, where it exists, is not uniformly distributed over the condition channels.
Contact-moment forcing (P1, calibrated onset pattern) yields only $7\%$ even for the
best-conditioned model (Table~\ref{tab:bypass}, last column): the 1--2-frame contact
\emph{transient} is weakly adopted across the board. The state-transplant probe (P2) shows what
is adopted instead. Transplanting a measured first-contact state into pre-contact descent frames
moves the \emph{naive policy} by $105\%$ of its descent command---the naive policy is not
force-blind; its raw route responds strongly to force-bearing state. But transplanting a
\emph{sealed} state moves both models far more than the contact state does (on-robot: $147\%$
vs.\ $67\%$ for the naive policy; Sec.~\ref{sec:robot-live}), and the strongest single
responses everywhere are to the stable, sustained signals---seal and sustained press---rather
than to contact onset.

The picture: what a policy adopts is decided by the \emph{statistical character} of the signal.
Stable, persistent conditions (seal state) are adopted essentially for free; a transient that is
redundant with depth-like cues in ordinary demonstrations is not. This has an operational
corollary developed in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}: on stiff contact, even a policy with
substantial condition authority cannot succeed by \emph{reactive} gating at the contact
moment---the successful mechanism is anticipatory behavior plus post-hoc state gating, which is
exactly what the probes measure as adopted.

\subsection{Ablating the recipe: each ingredient is necessary}
\label{sec:offline-quintuple}

The section's central experiment removes the recipe's ingredients one at a time while holding
everything else fixed. Starting from the \emph{same} trained naive checkpoint, on the
\emph{same} demonstrations, we continue training four policies: the naive policy itself; FiLM
\emph{without} the mask (removes the bottleneck's exclusivity); FiLM with mask but
\emph{shuffled} \chat{} during training (removes the grounding: the bottleneck exists, its
content is noise); and the full recipe. Imitation error on held-out episodes is
indistinguishable across all four ($0.84$--$0.96$\,mm per step): on the data manifold, these
are the same policy. Under intervention they are three different mechanisms
(Table~\ref{tab:quintuple}, Fig.~\ref{fig:doseresponse}).

\textbf{Grounding, not capacity, is the active ingredient.} The ungrounded control kills every
alternative explanation at once: it has the same added parameters, the same continued training,
the same bottleneck---and zero response at every dose, with the worst closed-loop outcome in the
study ($431$\,mm penetration). An ungrounded bottleneck is \emph{worse} than raw access: the mask
removed the raw route and the noise-trained \chat{} replaced it with nothing. Conversely, the
unmasked control shows that adding the FiLM module without the mask changes nothing: its
dose-response curve lies on the naive policy's ($+1.57\rightarrow+1.12$ vs.\
$+1.41\rightarrow+1.12$; identical at $12$\,N), its pathway decomposition puts the response
entirely on the raw route, and its loss matches the grounded variant's to the fourth decimal
($0.14750$ vs.\ $0.14762$)---the bypass finding, replicated with authority now at $94\%$ vs.\
$0$.

\textbf{The form dissociation.} The naive policy responds to force---at the demonstrated
magnitude. As the dose rises past the demonstrations, its response \emph{fades}
($36\%\rightarrow28\%$): a template, matched ever more poorly as reality departs from it. The
grounded bottleneck's response \emph{grows monotonically}, crossing full cancellation into
retreat at $12$\,N---where the unsaturated channel reads five times its training range. The
monotone extrapolation is not learned from data (no demonstration presses to these forces); it
is inherited from the channel's design as a calibrated monotone scalar. Same data, same
imitation: raw access binds a template, the grounded bottleneck binds a brake.

\textbf{Checkpoint robustness, and what the naive policy's stopping is bound to.} The forms are
not artifacts of checkpoint selection: no naive checkpoint we examined exceeds $65\%$
cancellation at any dose---later checkpoints respond more in open loop yet fare \emph{worse} in
the closed-loop press simulation, because a response that saturates below full cancellation
cannot stop a stiff press regardless of its size---while the grounded brake remains monotone to
the end of training. Finally, the seal-granted control variant of P4 localizes the naive
policy's failure: given a seal event at shallow depth it stops normally ($0.5$\,mm over-travel);
withhold the seal and it presses through. Its stopping is real but bound to the \emph{seal
event}; the grounded policy's is bound to \emph{force}.

\subsection{Scale does not change the class}
\label{sec:offline-pi05}

Fine-tuning $\pi_{0.5}$ ($3.6$B parameters, $8\times$ SmolVLA)~\cite{pi05} on the same
demonstrations reproduces the naive-policy signature: strong on-manifold transplant response
($65\%$), a dose-response that saturates shallowly (never exceeding $37\%$ cancellation,
approaching no full stop at any dose), and closed-loop collapse in the seal-never press
simulation ($0/6$, max penetration $86$\,mm---worse than the smaller model), at equal-or-better
imitation error. Neither parameter count nor a different VLA architecture converts template
responding into force-bound braking; the failure class is a property of the objective-plus-data,
not of model scale.
